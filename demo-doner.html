04. The Layers

Seven layers.
Zero filler.

LAYER / 01 — THE MEAT

Roasted Veal

Hand-stacked with lamb on the vertical spit. 4–6 hours at heat renders and bastes the layers beneath. Sliced thin, to order.

LAYER / 02 — THE BASE

Durum Flatbread

Stone-baked daily. Durum wheat dough, thin-rolled and dry-toasted on a cast iron plate. Crisp edges, soft centre. Never chilled.

LAYER / 03 — THE FRESH

Heirloom Tomato

Sliced thick, salted at the pass. It cools the heat of the meat and carries the juice through every layer below it.

LAYER / 04 — THE CUT

Sumac Onions

Red onions shaved paper-thin, dusted with sumac. The sharp, citrus edge that cuts through the richness of the veal.

LAYER / 05 — THE SAUCE

Garlic Yogurt

Whipped daily with roasted garlic and a whisper of lemon. Cool first, warm finish — the layer that binds the stack.

LAYER / 06+7 — THE STACK

Sliced To Order

Every portion shaved at the moment you order it — never held, never reheated. Layers six and seven are heat and timing.

The Roast — On The Spit

The spit rests.
The fire doesn't.

Four to six hours of slow rotation over charcoal. The outside sears, the inside bastes itself, layer by layer. When the knife finally touches it, the work is already done — the slice is just the reveal.

THE STORY — 01 / 03 · ISTANBUL 🇹🇷

The recipe is older than the sandwich

Vertical roasting was perfected in Ottoman kitchens — meat stacked, turned slowly against the fire, shaved as it crisped. That patience is our foundation.

THE STORY — 02 / 03 · BERLIN 🇩🇪

1972 — the sandwich is born

In Kreuzberg, the spit met the bread. Meat, salad and sauce folded into flatbread for workers on the move — a street icon was engineered by necessity.

THE STORY — 03 / 03 · TODAY

Two worlds. One spec.

We kept the soul of both cities and rebuilt the döner like a product spec: dry-aged meat, measured heat, seven layers, zero filler. Tradition — with tolerances.
